Workouts by age range

General starting points, not hard rules — plenty of people fall outside their bracket's typical pattern. Use these as a starting filter, then adjust based on how your body actually responds.

13–19

Teens

Movement habits matter more than intensity right now. Bodyweight training and light resistance work build a foundation without overloading still-developing joints.

20–39

Adults

Generally the body's peak decade for recovery and intensity. Higher-effort training — heavier strength work, HIIT, longer sessions — tends to be well tolerated here.

40–59

Midlife

Cardiovascular health becomes a bigger priority, and recovery often takes a little longer. Steady endurance work paired with strength training supports both.

60+

Older adults

Balance, joint mobility, and fall prevention move to the front of the line. Lower-impact mobility work and controlled strength training support everyday independence.

These are general starting points, not medical advice — check with a doctor before starting a new program, especially with an existing condition or injury.
